What it shows

A honeycomb lattice of point masses. Nearest neighbours are joined by Morse bonds (or plain harmonic springs), second neighbours by springs that supply the shear rigidity a central-force honeycomb completely lacks — without them the lattice is a mechanism and collapses. Colour shows the strain carried by each bond.

With bond kinetics switched on, a bond breaks past a set extension and can re-form — but only between atoms that were neighbours to begin with, and never past a valence of three. That is a distance rule, not chemistry: it produces plausible tearing and healing, and it should not be read as a prediction about real defects.

How to use

  • Set the lattice size and press Rebuild. The size slider is logarithmic: 5, 50 and 500 cells at left, centre and right.
  • Kick adds disorder to an already-running lattice; the initial disorder slider sets what a fresh build starts with.
  • Turn damping down to watch energy conserve, up to watch it settle.
  • Drag on the lattice to grab an atom and drive it.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space run / pause   S step   K kick
  • R reset   B rebuild   D dark   I this panel

Accuracy & limitations

The equilibrium is exact — a relaxed lattice does not move, to machine precision — and energy is conserved to better than 0.01 % without damping. What this is not is a bond-order potential: coordination costs nothing, edges are not reconstructed, and the bond list is fixed at build time. For anything chemical, use AIREBO in the molecular-dynamics plugin.
