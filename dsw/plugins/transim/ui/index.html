Scattering & tracer

ℓ (given, or from µ and n) is the TRANSPORT mean free path for every angular law, so a given mobility means the same thing whichever law you pick: a small-angle law scatters more often, every ℓ·(1−⟨cos Δθ⟩), to relax momentum as fast. σ is used by the Gaussian law, p by the partly specular edge. Diffuse reflection follows Lambert's cosine law.

Acoustic phonons: an isotropic channel of length ℓ_ph(300 K)·300/T, added by Matthiessen's rule to the trajectories AND to the FEM mobility, so the two models stay comparable. µ above is then the impurity-limited (low-temperature) mobility.

Electron–electron collisions as one-body small-angle kicks (the Klein magnetometer model). Real e–e collisions conserve total momentum, so this channel is not counted in the mobility and is not given to the FEM; treat it as an indication, not a hydrodynamic model.

Exact arcs solve every wall, contact and scattering event analytically (no step error) and need a uniform density; with a density map loaded the stepped tracer runs instead, and the result says which one ran. Refraction (stepped tracer, density map) bends rays toward higher |n| as Snell's law requires, so a density step totally reflects rays beyond the critical angle. Where the density map changes sign (a p–n junction, e.g. a map from −2e12 to +2e12) a carrier tunnels with the abrupt-junction Klein probability T = cos θ₁ cos θ₂ / cos²((θ₁+θ₂)/2) and leaves on the same side of the normal (negative refraction), or is reflected. A symmetric junction (equal |n| on both sides) refocuses a point source at the mirror distance: a Veselago lens. A junction width d > 0 uses the smooth-step law of Cayssol, Huard & Goldhaber-Gordon (PRB 79, 075428), which filters oblique rays more and more strongly as d grows (k_F d ≈ 1 at d ≈ 5.6 nm for 10¹² cm⁻²). The width is a physical parameter; the drawn map only sets where the junction is. Density maps are read at up to 256 pixels.

Viscous (hydrodynamic) flow

In clean graphene electron-electron collisions conserve momentum and the electrons flow like a viscous fluid (Stokes-Ohm model). Choose it here; the FEM and Hybrid buttons then solve it instead of Ohm's law.

Model assumptions

Trajectories are semiclassical and phase-incoherent; p–n junctions use the Dirac (Klein) transmission per angle. The field model is either local drift–diffusion (FEM) or a viscous electron fluid (hydro). The crossover sweep model adds Sharvin and Drude resistances in series. Not included: quantum interference, Landau quantisation, the Dirac-point minimum conductivity, contact resistance.
