What it shows

A twisted bilayer — two live layers, each a honeycomb of Morse bonds with second-neighbour springs and a bending term — resting on a rigid substrate through Lennard-Jones forces. A fixed-N gas pocket can be placed either between the layers or below the whole bilayer, which is the distinction this plugin exists for: in the first case only the top layer lifts, in the second the pair moves together and bends as a stiffer composite membrane.

The gas is not simulated as molecules. Its pressure follows p = NkT/V from the volume the layer actually encloses, so inflating the bubble lowers the pressure — the feedback that makes a blister an equilibrium object rather than a runaway. The pressurised footprint follows the peel front instead of being a fixed disc, so the delamination radius is an output.

How to use

  • Set a twist and rebuild, then Admit gas and Run.
  • Switch Placement between and below to compare — everything else identical.
  • Colour by Registry for the moiré, by Strain for the distortion. The compression and shear rings sit at the peel front.
  • Drag to rotate, wheel to zoom, tilt 90° for plan view.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space run / pause   S single step
  • G admit or stop gas   R reset   B rebuild
  • C cycle colour mode   D dark   I this panel

Accuracy & limitations

The in-plane model is a toy: Morse bonds plus springs, not a bond-order potential. It is reliable for membrane mechanics — blister shapes, draping, registry — and it is not chemistry: there is no bond breaking here, and nothing about defects or edges should be trusted. The ideal gas law is used well outside its range at GPa pressures; what survives is the stiffness p ∝ 1/V, not the molecule count.
