How the tracker works
  • Tapping the marker builds a colour model from the surrounding patch: the hue-like ratios r:g:b, which barely change when the light does, plus how much the patch colour varies.
  • Each frame the tracker predicts where the marker went from its last velocity, and searches only a small window around that prediction.
  • Matching pixels are grouped into connected blobs. The blob closest to the prediction and closest in size to the marker wins, so a second red object elsewhere in the frame cannot drag the measurement away.
  • If nothing is found the window grows, the colour tolerance is relaxed step by step, and finally the whole frame is searched – so a briefly hidden marker is picked up again automatically.
  • The colour model and the expected blob size drift slowly with the light, but are held near the colour you originally tapped so the tracker cannot creep onto the background.
  • The analysis resolution is tuned to the speed of your phone, and frames are sampled as the camera delivers them, which keeps the time axis honest.
  • Anti-shake reference: tap a second, stationary coloured point and its motion is subtracted, removing most of the effect of a hand-held phone.
Limitations
  • Position is measured in pixels, not meters.
  • Camera motion looks like pendulum motion – hold the phone still, use a support, or set an anti-shake reference.
  • Strong lighting changes or a marker colour close to the background will still confuse colour tracking.
  • Velocity and especially acceleration amplify camera noise.
  • Camera frame rate limits temporal resolution.
  • This is a simple educational measurement tool, not a calibrated laboratory instrument.
