How it works

The image is reduced to one number per grid cell: how much printed material sits there. Anything on the positive side of the threshold becomes solid, the rest becomes background. That field is turned into a height map in millimetres, textured, and then walked cell by cell into a watertight triangle mesh — top surface, vertical walls, flat bottom — written out as a binary STL that any slicer will open.

Texture

Grain is fractal noise over the whole top surface: a cast, sandblasted or stone-like finish. Edge chew is added before the threshold, so the noise bites into the outline itself rather than the surface — this is what makes a crisp logo look eaten and hand-cut. Edge wear knocks the height down near boundaries, so the top edges arrive chipped rather than machined. Scratches, pits and cracks are carved as strokes and stamps into the height field. Everything is driven by the seed, so the same settings give the same model every time — press Reroll for a different roll of the same dice.

Printing

Keep the relief height above about two layer heights or it will vanish in slicing, and keep the finest texture wider than your nozzle — a 0.4 mm nozzle cannot draw a 0.15 mm scratch, though it will still show as a dent. The minimum wall setting stops texture from cutting through the base. Cut-out models have no backing plate, so make sure the artwork is one connected piece before printing: floating islands print as separate parts.
