How it works

Playing it

Photo 1 is the oscillator: drag a finger over it and the colour under the fingertip becomes a tone. Photo 2 is the filter: touch it and the colour there shapes that tone. Both pads work at the same time — one finger on each.

Latch leaves the cursor where you lifted your finger. On the tone pad the note keeps sounding; on the filter pad the filter setting is held. Tap again to move the cursor, or turn latch off to release.

Colour → tone (photo 1)

  • Hue → pitch. With the default spectral law the hue is read as a wavelength (red ≈ 700 nm through to violet ≈ 380 nm) and the pitch follows the photon energy E = hc/λ: semitones ∝ log(700 nm / λ). Red is the lowest note, violet the highest, and the whole visible band is stretched over the range you choose.
  • Saturation → harmonic richness. Partial n starts at n−p with p = 3.4 − 2.6·S: a washed-out grey is nearly a pure sine, a vivid colour is a full harmonic stack (ten partials).
  • Warm vs cool → odd/even balance. Reds thin out the even harmonics for a hollow, clarinet-like tone; blues keep the complete series and sound brighter.
  • Green → a formant bump on the third to fifth partial, which adds a vocal edge.
  • Value → loudness. Dark corners of the photo are quiet, highlights are loud.

Colour → filter (photo 2)

  • Value → cut-off, from 80 Hz in the shadows to 14 kHz in the highlights, so a dark patch mutes the tone and a bright one opens it up.
  • Saturation → resonance (Q), from a gentle 0.6 to a whistling 15.
  • Hue → a resonant peak swept from 180 Hz to 6 kHz by the same wavelength law, with its height set by saturation. Grey pixels leave the sound uncoloured.

The strip above the settings draws it: the bars are the harmonics of the current tone, the curve is the filter your second finger is describing, and the solid bars are what survives.

Photos

Camera opens the camera, Photos opens the camera roll (on iOS both live behind the same picker, so either button gets you there). Nothing is uploaded — the photos are decoded and read in the browser and never leave the phone. Demo loads a synthetic spectrum chart if you just want to try it.

On an iPhone the browser follows the ring/silent switch for web audio: if the pads look alive but you hear nothing, flip the switch or turn the volume up. Add the page to the home screen for a full-screen instrument.
