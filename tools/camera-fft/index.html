How to read this
  • The camera image is cropped to a square and reduced to N×N pixels; the spectrum below is its 2D Fourier transform.
  • The centre of the spectrum is zero spatial frequency (DC); the edges are the Nyquist limit, 0.5 cycles per pixel.
  • Stripes in the image give a pair of bright spots, placed perpendicular to the stripes. Finer stripes sit further from the centre.
  • The mean is subtracted and a Hann window applied before transforming, so the DC peak and the edge-wrap cross do not swamp the plot.
  • Brightness is a log scale set by the gain slider — it changes the display only, not the data.
  • The red, green and blue buttons analyse that colour channel alone; white uses luminance (0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B).
  • This is a teaching visualiser: the axis is cycles per pixel, not a calibrated spatial frequency in mm-1.
