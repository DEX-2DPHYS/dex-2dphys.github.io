Inside the double pendulum

Two swings, joined together

A double pendulum is simply one pendulum hanging from another. The orange mass pulls on the blue mass, and the blue mass pulls back. That connection turns two familiar swings into one surprisingly rich motion.

Try a gentle releaseSmall displacements often produce smooth, repeating motion.
Try a forceful throwMore energy opens many competing paths through the same space.
The fading trails show where each mass has recently been. The lower trace shows the blue mass's horizontal position, x(t), changing with time.

Energy moves between the links

Neither arm has its own independent rhythm. Motion of the upper arm moves the pivot of the lower arm; motion of the lower arm pushes back through the joint. Energy continually shifts between height, speed, and the two links.

Potential energyRaised masses store gravitational energy.
Kinetic energyMoving masses carry energy through both rotations.

This is nonlinear coupling: doubling an angle does not simply double the response. The geometry itself changes the forces as the pendulum moves.

Deterministic, yet unpredictable

The pendulum obeys precise equations—there is no randomness in the simulation. Yet two releases that differ by an almost invisible amount can eventually follow completely different paths. This is sensitive dependence on initial conditions.

δ(t)≈δ0eλt

Here δ0 is a tiny initial separation and λ is a Lyapunov exponent. When λ>0, nearby motions separate exponentially: practical long-term prediction becomes impossible.

Chaos is not disorder. It is structured motion whose details become extremely sensitive to how accurately the starting state is known.

What this simulation is doing

The equations are advanced in tiny time steps with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method. A small damping term gradually removes energy, representing losses that a real pivot and air resistance would introduce.

At the same time, an invisible “shadow” pendulum starts only one millionth of a unit away. The app repeatedly measures how fast that shadow separates from the visible pendulum.
