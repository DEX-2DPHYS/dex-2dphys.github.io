Ten passages taken off the frame during the survey, five from each object, thirty seconds each. Every one of them moves a quantity across its travel rather than setting it and leaving it there — which is the method the report ends by recommending, and the only one that found anything. The names are the ones the survey used among itself; the designation and the journal reference follow.

B2311.22 · OBS-07

Two Voices

named in the field by T. Okonkwo-Reyes, hydrologist

The same body disturbed at five places: its most connected site, its most remote, a still press held until it deepens, a circling contact that accumulates, and a fast stroke let go.

Contact locus and modal weighting in a sectioned network body. Field journal 7, pp. 41–46; specimen log 2311/0022. Spectra differing by 0.709 in relative terms; the centre of the sound at 362 Hz on the connected site and 228 Hz on the remote.

B2311.22 · OBS-11

The Long Walk Down

named in the field by M. Halvard, recording

One disturbance, put into a short stretch of the body and then left entirely alone, at five rates of redistribution.

Intermodal transport under a single sustained disturbance. Field journal 7, pp. 88–97. At rest the centre of the energy holds still, 526 Hz to 522 Hz over four seconds. At the far end it travels 450 Hz to 213 Hz, and the sound is no quieter at the end of it.

B2311.22 · OBS-16

The Clock in the Jar

named in the field by E. Charbonneau, frame

Five recurrence intervals, from four seconds down to one and a half. In between them, states are audible that belong to neither the beginning nor the end.

Exact periodicity of output under one configuration of the frame. Field journal 8, pp. 12–19. Reassembly at a correlation of 0.998 to 0.999 at every interval offered.

B2311.22 · OBS-22

Talking to Strangers

named in the field by J. Vashti-Lund, spectrometry

One body put in a vessel with five others in turn, from itself to the least related body the survey could reach.

Pairwise absorption and re-emission; ladder overlap across the catalogue. Field journal 8, pp. 51–63. Overlap 1.000 against itself and 0.059 against the last; the reply seventeen times stronger at the near end than the far. The third set of frequencies, which neither body can make alone, is a sixth of what is heard throughout.

B2311.22 · OBS-29

What It Keeps

named in the field by T. Okonkwo-Reyes, hydrologist

The responding body alone as catalogued; then eighteen seconds in a vessel with another; then the same body alone again, at rest, with nothing having been done to it.

Irreversible spectral displacement in a responding body. Field journal 9, pp. 4–22. 53.9 cents of displacement in nineteen discrete steps. Afterwards it overlaps its own catalogue entry at 0.471, and it does not go back.

B2311.67 · OBS-34

Sixty-Seven Grains

named in the field by K. Oyelaran, geometry

Five, twenty-nine, seventy, a hundred and sixty-nine and four hundred and eight sites of the lattice sounding at once.

Spectral measure against extent; sections taken at Pell lengths. Field journal 11, pp. 30–44. The largest gap in the spectrum runs from 2.7 times the median at five sites to 659 times it at four hundred and eight: the gaps subdividing rather than filling in.

B2311.67 · OBS-38

Opening the Shutter

named in the field by E. Charbonneau, frame

The window opened in five steps from a twelfth of its travel to all of it, which is where the body is a section of the lattice and not a thinned one.

Site density and bond mixture against the acceptance window. Field journal 11, pp. 71–80. Density from 0.27 to 1.32 sites per unit length; three bond lengths narrowing to two at the full window, with the long standing to the short at 2.414 — the ratio native to eightfold order.

B2311.67 · OBS-44

It Spoke in Our Language

named in the field by M. Halvard, recording

The section tilted against the lattice through four angles, one of which is not a tilt the body will take, and one of which leaves it an ordinary crystal.

Approach to a harmonic series under tilt of the section. Field journal 12, pp. 9–28. 47.4 cents from the nearest harmonic series unstrained; 8.2 cents at a Pell tilt; and 59.0 cents at a tilt that is not one, which carries it further off rather than nearer.

B2311.67 · OBS-47

Two Rooms

named in the field by J. Vashti-Lund, spectrometry

The chain alone, then the visible body admitted a little, then further, then alone and drifting in the fourth dimension, then both together with the distant orders dying first.

The direct-space channel against the reciprocal. Field journal 12, pp. 44–58. Fourteen to forty-seven orders heard. The chain persists; the star is struck, rings and dies, which is why the observer is watching one thing and listening to another.

B2311.67 · OBS-52

The Wiring Wakes

named in the field by K. Oyelaran, geometry

The same specimen at liquid nitrogen, at 200 K, at room temperature, at 500 K and at 800 K, with nothing touched between one and the next.

Temperature-driven cross-coupling, 77 K to 800 K, specimen 041. Field journal 13, pp. 2–31. At 77 K nothing moves at all. At 800 K nine quantities are stirred by the specimen's own arrangement — among them the acceptance window, which belongs to the frame and not to the material.
